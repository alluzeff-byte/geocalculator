Tidal levels explained

TPXO10
TPXO is a family of global barotropic tidal models developed at Oregon State University (OSU). 8 primary harmonic constituents (via ODB's Open Tide API) run through a 19-year astronomical simulation, computed directly in your browser. No key needed.
NOC CS3 / NOC CS20
Product from the National Oceanography Centre. CS3 is their broader NW Europe model; CS20 is a higher-resolution model for specific licensed areas - which one(s) you have access to depends on your key. Both models provide level and depth-averaged current prediction. This choice only affects the Series plot below, not the statistics above - NOC's statistics function always picks its own model, regardless of which one you select here. Requires an API key, entered above (defaults to NOC's public demo key, which only covers a small area of the Bristol Channel/Celtic Sea and is CS20-only; your own key is stored only in this browser).
HAT — Highest Astronomical Tide
The highest level the tide is predicted to reach under average meteorological conditions, for any combination of astronomical conditions.
MHWS — Mean High Water Springs
The average height of the high waters occurring at spring tides (when the Sun and Moon's tidal effects reinforce each other).
MHWN — Mean High Water Neaps
The average height of the high waters occurring at neap tides (when the Sun and Moon's tidal effects partly cancel).
MSL — Mean Sea Level
The average level of the sea surface. All other levels here are expressed relative to this (MSL = 0).
MLWN — Mean Low Water Neaps
The average height of the low waters occurring at neap tides.
MLWS — Mean Low Water Springs
The average height of the low waters occurring at spring tides.
LAT — Lowest Astronomical Tide
The lowest level the tide is predicted to reach under average meteorological conditions, for any combination of astronomical conditions.
Tide type
The form number F = (K1+O1)/(M2+S2), a ratio of the main diurnal to semi-diurnal constituent amplitudes. F ≤ 0.25 is semi-diurnal (two similar high/low tides a day); 0.25–1.5 is mixed, mainly semi-diurnal; 1.5–3.0 is mixed, mainly diurnal; above 3.0 is diurnal (one high/low tide a day).
Parameter: Level / Current
Level shows the 8 tidal heights above. Current shows the same point's tidal current instead - a single depth-averaged value (not a 3D profile), from the same NOC data already fetched for Level, just previously unused. TPXO doesn't provide current data in this app, so Current requires NOC CS3 or NOC CS20.
Max speed / Min speed
The strongest and weakest current speed predicted over a tidal cycle.
Dir @ max / Dir @ min
The current's direction (degrees, clockwise from true North) at the moments of maximum and minimum speed.
Eccentricity
How elliptical the current's path is over a tidal cycle, from 0 (perfectly circular, e.g. far offshore where the current rotates through all directions) to 1 (perfectly back-and-forth/rectilinear, typical in a narrow channel).
Datum (Series only)
Which vertical reference the plotted height curve is measured against: MSL (default, 0 = mean sea level, same as the statistics above) or LAT (0 = Lowest Astronomical Tide, so the curve never goes negative). Only affects the Level curve on the Series plot - has no effect on the statistics above (which are always MSL-referenced by definition) or on Parameter=Current (current speed/direction don't depend on a height reference). Works for both TPXO and NOC CS3/CS20; for TPXO this runs its 19-year simulation a second time to find the LAT offset, so switching to LAT is noticeably slower than MSL.
Unit
Metric (default: m, m/s) or Imperial (ft, kt) - converts both the statistics above and the Series plot, for whichever quantity is currently shown (heights or current speed). Direction and Eccentricity are unaffected either way. Switching Unit re-displays the last result immediately, without needing to press Calculate again.
Collecting statistics
After a Calculate, drag any of the readout lines above out past the edge of the panel to drop the whole set of statistics into a small card captioned with its location and model. Each drag adds another card - they lay out in rows and columns beside the calculator, so several locations/models can be compared at once; every card has its own Clear and Export (.xlsx, with location/model as columns).
Format
TPXO10 (via ODB Open Tide API) · 8 primary constituents, 19-year harmonic simulation · all levels relative to MSL

Meteo sources explained

ERA5
ECMWF's global atmospheric reanalysis, served via Open-Meteo's historical archive (1940–present, hourly, ~9–31 km resolution). Covers air temperature, humidity, air pressure, wind and cloud cover.
NORA3
MET Norway's regional atmospheric hindcast (1959–present, hourly, ~3 km resolution, Lambert conformal grid over Northern Europe waters). Adds wind speed at multiple heights (10–750 m) alongside temperature, humidity, pressure, cloud fraction and precipitation.
NCEI Obs
NOAA's Global Historical Climatology Network (GHCN-Daily) – real observed daily readings from actual weather stations worldwide, not a model. Automatically finds and uses the nearest station that records the selected parameter, so coverage and period of record vary by location.
IEM Obs
Iowa Environmental Mesonet's archive of real ASOS/AWOS airport weather station reports, worldwide, at routine METAR reporting frequency (~hourly, typically :50 past the hour – special/high-frequency reports are excluded so resolution stays consistent everywhere). Covers temperature, precipitation, cloud cover/base height, wind speed/gust and relative humidity/pressure – variables that GHCN's climate-network stations rarely carry – using the nearest airport station to the selected point. Precipitation and peak gust are reliably reported mainly at US stations.
Met Office Obs
37 long-running UK stations from the Met Office's historic station data archive, monthly, back to as early as 1853 – the longest record here by far, though narrower (max/min temperature, air frost days, rainfall, sunshine) and UK-only. Pick a station directly from the Saved Location dropdown, which lists these 37 stations by name while this Source is selected. Routed through a third-party CORS proxy since the Met Office's own files don't support direct browser access – occasional failures are possible if that proxy is unavailable.

Weather sources explained

Met Office
Live observations from the Met Office's own synoptic weather stations (UK, Channel Islands, Isle of Man, plus a few Met Éireann stations), via the Met Office Weather DataHub Land Observations API. Stations report hourly, and the latest report is shown with its observation time (UTC) below the readings. Clicking a value charts the last 48 hours; the chart's Period menu can shorten that to 24 hours. Pick a station from the Station dropdown, or click Map to choose one of the orange dots. The station list is a local snapshot of 105 stations – the Met Office quotes about 150, so a few may be missing.
Bright Sky (DWD)
Live observations from about 950 stations across Europe – the German weather service's (DWD) own network in Germany (about 525 stations) plus the international SYNOP stations DWD collects from other countries – via Bright Sky (brightsky.dev), a free, open-source API over DWD's open data. No key or daily limit. Hourly reports for the last 48 hours – 96 hours for stations in Germany, where DWD's own archive fills in the earlier two days; the latest is shown, and clicking a value charts the whole period (the chart's Period menu can shorten it to 24 or 48 hours). Only measured values are shown: where Bright Sky would fill a gap from a forecast, that value is left blank. Bright Sky has no pressure-tendency or weather-code fields, so Pressure tendency is worked out from the change in pressure over the last 3 hours, and Present weather is Bright Sky's own summary of the hour (Clear, Cloudy, Rain, Fog…). Humidity, when a station doesn't report it, is calculated from temperature and dew point. The station list is a local snapshot (October 2026).
DMI (Denmark)
Live observations from the Danish Meteorological Institute's own stations – 114 in Denmark, Greenland and the Faroe Islands – via DMI's free open-data API (no key or daily limit). Stations report every 10 minutes; the readout shows the newest 10-minute values, and clicking a value charts the last 96 hours at each full hour – the Period menu in the chart's top-left corner switches between 24 and 48 hours and 7, 14, 30, 90, 180 or 365 days (markers start switched off for 30 days and longer; a year takes several seconds to load). Wind gust is the highest gust in the past hour. Present weather is DMI's weather code where one was reported (mainly when there is precipitation, fog etc.), otherwise a description of the cloud cover (Clear, Partly cloudy, Cloudy, Overcast). Pressure tendency is worked out from the change in pressure over the last 3 hours.
MeteoSwiss
Live observations from SwissMetNet, MeteoSwiss's automatic network – 149 stations in Switzerland and Liechtenstein – from MeteoSwiss open data (no key or daily limit). The readout shows the newest 10-minute values; clicking a value charts the last 96 hours as hourly values (hourly means; gust = the hour's peak gust), and the chart's Period menu switches between 24 and 48 hours and 7, 14, 30, 90, 180 or 365 days. Periods back to 1 January load instantly; reaching further back first downloads the station's archive file for the decade (several MB). These stations don't measure visibility or present weather, so those show “—”. Air pressure is QFF (reduced to sea level using the actual temperature) where the station measures it, otherwise QNH (reduced using a standard atmosphere), which can differ by a few hPa at mountain stations. Pressure tendency is worked out from the change in pressure over the last 3 hours.
GeoSphere (Austria)
Live observations from GeoSphere Austria's TAWES network of automatic stations – 286 stations, grouped by state – via GeoSphere's free open-data API (no key or daily limit). Stations report every 10 minutes; the readout shows the newest values, and clicking a value charts the last 96 hours at each full hour (gust = the highest gust in that hour), with the same Period menu as DMI (24 hours to 365 days). GeoSphere's 10-minute network keeps only about 3 months, so 180 and 365 days come from its hourly quality-checked climate archive instead (not available for 5 Innsbruck city stations). These stations don't report visibility or present weather. Pressure is reduced to sea level; pressure tendency is worked out from the change over the last 3 hours.
MET Norway
Observations from about 1,100 stations in Norway and Svalbard, grouped by county – MET Norway's own stations plus partner networks (road-weather stations, airports, offshore platforms…) – via MET Norway's Frost API, reached through a small proxy (Frost needs a client ID and doesn't allow direct browser access). The readout shows each station's newest values; clicking a value charts hourly values for the last 96 hours, with the same Period menu as DMI (24 hours to 365 days). Many partner stations measure only temperature and humidity, and only some report sea-level pressure; visibility and present weather aren't available. Pressure tendency is worked out from the change over the last 3 hours. Data: MET Norway, CC BY 4.0.
SMHI (Sweden)
Hourly observations from about 230 stations of SMHI, the Swedish Meteorological and Hydrological Institute, grouped by county, via SMHI's free open-data API (no key or daily limit). The latest hourly report is shown; clicking a value charts the last 96 hours, with the same Period menu as DMI (24 hours to 365 days). The last 4 months load quickly; 180 and 365 days also download the station's quality-checked archive for each charted value (several seconds). Not every station measures everything – gust, visibility and present weather are reported at about two-thirds of them. Wind gust is the hour's highest gust. Present weather is SMHI's weather code where one was reported, otherwise a description of the cloud cover. Pressure tendency is worked out from the change over the last 3 hours.
FMI (Finland)
Observations from about 180 automatic stations of FMI, the Finnish Meteorological Institute, grouped by region, via FMI's free open-data service (no key). Stations report every 10 minutes; the readout shows the newest values, and clicking a value charts the last 96 hours at each full hour, with the same Period menu as DMI (24 hours to 365 days – FMI only serves a week per request, so a year takes 53 requests and about 15 seconds). Wind gust is the highest 10-minute gust in the hour. Present weather is the station's automatic weather code where there is something to report, otherwise the cloud cover. Pressure tendency is worked out from the change over the last 3 hours.
Vedur.is (Iceland)
Observations from about 280 automatic stations in Iceland, grouped by forecast region – the Icelandic Met Office's (Veðurstofa Íslands) own stations plus those of the Road Administration and others – via IMO's free weather API (no key). The readout shows the newest 10-minute values; clicking a value charts hourly values for the last 96 hours, with the same Period menu as DMI (24 hours to 365 days). Each hourly value covers the hour ending at its time, and gust is that hour's highest. Many road stations don't measure pressure, and none of these stations report visibility or present weather. Pressure tendency is worked out from the change over the last 3 hours.
KNMI (Netherlands)
Observations from 70 stations of KNMI, the Royal Netherlands Meteorological Institute – land stations, North Sea platforms and the Caribbean Netherlands (Bonaire, Saba, Sint Eustatius) – via the KNMI Data Platform's EDR API, reached through the same small proxy as MET Norway (KNMI needs an API key and doesn't allow direct browser access). Stations report every 10 minutes; the readout shows the newest values, and clicking a value charts the last 96 hours at each full hour, with the same Period menu as DMI (24 hours to 365 days). Wind gust is the highest 10-minute gust in the hour. Some coastal stations measure only wind. Present weather is the station's automatic weather code where there is something to report, otherwise the cloud cover. Pressure tendency is worked out from the change over the last 3 hours. The API key allows 1,000 requests per hour, shared by everyone using this page.
Météo-France
Hourly observations from about 2,070 Météo-France stations in metropolitan France and the overseas territories, grouped by département, via Météo-France's public observation API, reached through the same small proxy as MET Norway (it needs an API key). Reports appear about 10 minutes after each hour; the latest is shown, and clicking a value charts the last 96 hours – Météo-France's real-time service keeps only about 4 days, so the chart's Period menu offers 24, 48 or 96 hours. Not every station measures everything: many measure only temperature (and rain); about 40% also report wind and humidity, and only about 1 in 10 pressure or visibility. Wind gust is the hour's highest gust. There is no present-weather code, so Present weather describes the cloud cover where a station reports it. Pressure tendency is worked out from the change over the last 3 hours. The API key allows 100 requests per minute, shared by everyone using this page; one request covers every station in a département.
Met Éireann (Ireland)
Hourly observations from Met Éireann's 25 main (synoptic) stations, grouped by county, via the observations service behind met.ie (no key needed). The latest hourly report is shown; clicking a value charts today's and yesterday's reports – all the service offers, so up to 48 hours, with a Period menu of 24 or 48 hours. Met Éireann reports wind in km/h, converted here to m/s, with the direction to the nearest of 8 compass points; temperature, sea-level pressure and humidity are whole numbers. Wind gust is given only when there is one. There is no visibility. Present weather is Met Éireann's own description. Pressure tendency is worked out from the change over the last 3 hours. Data: © Met Éireann (CC BY 4.0).
RMI (Belgium)
Hourly observations from the 27 synoptic stations of the Royal Meteorological Institute of Belgium (KMI/IRM), grouped by province, via RMI's open-data service, reached through the same small proxy as MET Norway (the service doesn't allow direct browser requests). The latest hourly report is shown; clicking a value charts the last 96 hours, and the chart's Period menu reaches back up to 365 days (RMI's archive goes back to 1952 at Uccle). Wind speed and gust are in m/s, gust being the peak gust reported with each observation. Only about half the stations report humidity, and none report visibility. Present weather is the station's weather code where one is reported, otherwise the cloud cover. Pressure tendency is worked out from the change over the last 3 hours. Data: © RMI (open data).
AEMET (Spain)
Hourly observations from about 860 automatic stations of AEMET, Spain's State Meteorological Agency – mainland Spain, the Balearic and Canary Islands, Ceuta and Melilla – grouped by province, via AEMET OpenData, reached through the same small proxy as MET Norway (it needs an API key). The latest hourly report is shown; clicking a value charts the last 12 hours – all that AEMET's observation service keeps, so there is no Period menu. Wind gust is the hour's highest gust. Only about a quarter of the stations report sea-level pressure and about one in eight visibility; there is no present-weather code. Pressure tendency is worked out from the change over the last 3 hours. Data: © AEMET.
IMGW (Poland)
The latest hourly report from the 62 synoptic stations of IMGW-PIB, Poland's national weather service, via its free public-data API (no key). That API only provides each station's newest report – no history – so there is no chart for this source, and no pressure tendency. It doesn't report wind gust, visibility or present weather either. Pressure is reduced to sea level (mountain stations such as Kasprowy Wierch report none). Station positions come from WMO's station registry, since IMGW's API doesn't give them.
API key
Met Office only (the field is hidden for the other sources). The API needs a free Met Office DataHub key subscribed to Land Observations. A default key is built in; to use your own, register at datahub.metoffice.gov.uk and paste it into the key field (stored only in this browser – clear the field to return to the default). The free plan allows 360 requests per day, shared by everyone using the same key, and resets at 00:00 UTC. Re-selecting the same station within 10 minutes reuses the last reading instead of spending a request.
Air temperature
Air temperature at screen height (1.25–2 m), °C.
Min / max temp 24 h
The lowest and highest air temperature over the 24 hours up to the latest report, °C. Where a source reports each hour's own minimum and maximum (DMI, KNMI, FMI and GeoSphere via their 10-minute values, MeteoSwiss, MET Norway, Vedur.is, Météo-France, AEMET, and RMI's 12-hour extremes) those are used, so short peaks between the hourly readings count; otherwise it's the extremes of the hourly readings. AEMET keeps only 12 hours, so there it covers 12 hours; IMGW has no history, so it shows nothing. Clicking it charts, for each hour, the minimum (blue) and maximum (red) over the 24 hours up to it – starting 24 hours into the period, once a full day of reports lies behind it.
Dew point
The temperature to which the air would have to cool for dew (or fog) to form, °C – the closer to the air temperature, the more humid the air. Where a source doesn't report it (the Met Office, RMI, Met Éireann, IMGW and some stations elsewhere), it is worked out from the temperature and relative humidity (Magnus formula).
Humidity
Relative humidity, %.
Air pressure
Mean sea-level pressure (station pressure reduced to sea level), hPa.
Pressure tendency
How pressure has changed over the last 3 hours: Rising, Falling or Steady.
Wind speed / Wind gust
Mean wind speed and the highest gust at 10 m, m/s.
Wind direction
The direction the wind blows from. The Met Office reports it as one of 16 compass points, so the degrees shown are that point's centre (steps of 22.5°).
Visibility
Horizontal visibility, km.
Cloud base
Height of the lowest cloud base above the station, m – shown blank when there is no cloud or the station doesn't measure it. Reported by KNMI (ceilometer, converted from feet), DMI, SMHI and some MET Norway stations (often only every 6 hours there); the other sources don't provide it. Manned stations report it in bands (e.g. 300–600 m), given as the band's lower limit.
Snow depth
Total depth of snow on the ground, cm – the latest value from the last 36 hours, since many stations measure it only once a day (DMI and SMHI in the morning). Reported by DMI, SMHI, FMI, MET Norway, MeteoSwiss, GeoSphere, Météo-France and some AEMET stations; 0 means measured bare ground, blank means not measured.
Sea state (offshore stations)
An extra row, shown only for MET Norway's offshore platforms that measure the sea (about 35 in the North Sea, Norwegian Sea and Barents Sea): significant wave height Hs (the mean height of the highest third of the waves, m), the highest single wave (m), the peak period (the period of the most energetic waves, s), the mean period (s), the direction the waves come from (°) and the sea-surface temperature (°C). Values are the 10-minute reports on the hour. Not every platform measures all of them.
Precipitation 1 h
Rain, drizzle and melted snow or hail in the past hour, mm (1 mm = 1 litre per m²). Not available from the Met Office, RMI (its synoptic reports only give 6- or 12-hour totals) or IMGW.
Present weather
The Met Office's weather-type code for the hour (e.g. Cloudy, Light rain, Fog), shown as text; "(day)/(night)" variants are merged.

Wave models explained

ERA5
ECMWF's ERA5-Ocean wave reanalysis, served via Open-Meteo's historical marine archive (1940–present, global). The app reads Open-Meteo's daily maximum Hs and period values (not an hourly series), so Hs here is the highest single day in the period, not a period average. No wind-sea/swell breakdown.
NORA3
MET Norway's regional wave hindcast (1959–present, hourly, ~3 km resolution, rotated-pole grid over Northern Europe waters). Hs, Hmax, mean wave periods and wave direction, split into wind-sea and swell.
AMM15
UK Met Office regional wave reanalysis (1980–present, 3-hourly, ~1.5 km resolution, North West Shelf/Atlantic Margin: Ireland, North Sea, English Channel, Bay of Biscay), also run with WAVEWATCH III. Splits swell into two separate partitions (Hs_Swell1/Hs_Swell2) rather than one combined swell field.

Ocean models explained

GLORYS12V1
Copernicus Marine's GLORYS12V1 global reanalysis (1993–present, 1/12° resolution, 50 depth levels) for depth-resolved temperature, salinity and currents, plus dedicated satellite-based sea surface temperature (1982–2024) and sea surface salinity (1993–2024) products. Daily-mean values.
NWS MFC
Copernicus Marine's North West Shelf regional reanalysis (NWSHELF_MULTIYEAR_PHY_004_009, 1993–present, 7 km resolution, 24 depth levels) covering the North Sea, Irish Sea and English Channel — the same depth-resolved temperature/salinity/current fields as GLORYS12V1, but at higher regional resolution. No dedicated SST/SSS or sea-ice fields. Daily-mean values.
NORA3
MET Norway's NORA3 atmospheric hindcast (1959–present) only carries one ocean-relevant field: sea surface temperature, taken from the model's own forcing/analysis field rather than a dedicated ocean reanalysis.
